I'm a Ph.D. student in Industrial and Systems Engineering at Georgia Tech, with a minor in Machine Learning. I'm advised by Pinar Keskinocak.

My research is about decision-making under uncertainty when evaluations are expensive — stochastic submodular maximization, Bayesian optimization with Gaussian process surrogates, and large-scale simulation. I apply these methods to global health, in collaboration with the U.S. CDC.

Research

My work spans two streams. The first is simulation-based optimization under uncertainty: allocating a limited evaluation budget well when every evaluation is costly, through adaptive subsampling for submodular selection and Gaussian process surrogates over expensive simulations. The second is vaccine data analytics for global immunization policy: measuring what immunization indicators actually capture, and what drives coverage across countries.

Adaptive Subsampling for Fixed-Budget Stochastic Submodular Maximization
Xingjian Wang, et al.
In preparation, 2026
To be presented at INFORMS Annual Meeting, 2026

Allocating a limited evaluation budget adaptively lets us reach comparable solution quality with 70% fewer evaluations, for budgeted subset selection under cardinality constraints.

Coverage Trajectories Before and After Measles-Rubella Vaccine Introduction and Contextual Factors Associated with Coverage in Gavi-Eligible Countries: An Ecological Time-Series Analysis
Xingjian Wang, et al.
Pending CDC clearance, 2026

Across 60+ Gavi-eligible countries and 27 contextual factors, immunization outcomes differ systematically by the type of Gavi support received — suggesting eligibility policy should weigh more than national income.

Accuracy of DTP Proxies in Global Immunization Metrics
Xingjian Wang, et al.
In preparation, 2026

DTP1 and DTP3 are used as proxies for zero-dose and full immunization coverage. Using household survey data across low- and middle-income countries, we quantify how accurate those proxies actually are (>75% on average).

Quantifying the Impact of Proactive Community Case Management on Severe Malaria Cases Using Agent-Based Simulation
Xingjian Wang, Hannah K. Smalley, Julie R. Gutman, Pinar Keskinocak
Winter Simulation Conference, 2025

An agent-based model of a 4,000-person village over a two-year horizon, used to evaluate how proactive community case management changes severe malaria outcomes.

Modeling the Impact of Proactive Community Case Management on Reducing Confirmed Malaria Cases in Sub-Saharan African Countries
Yizhou Wang, Xingjian Wang, Brian Gurbaxani, Julie R. Gutman, Pinar Keskinocak, Hannah K. Smalley, Julie Thwing
American Journal of Tropical Medicine and Hygiene, 2024

Simulation evidence on how proactive community case management reduces confirmed malaria cases across Sub-Saharan African settings.
